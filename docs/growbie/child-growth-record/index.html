How to keep a simple child growth record

A clear record keeps the date, measurement, and context together. It can also make it easier to bring useful questions to a pediatric visit.

1

Record the exact date

Growth charts depend on a child's age at the time of each measurement. Save the full date instead of a loose note such as “this month.”

2

Keep the method with the number

Note whether a measurement came from home or a medical visit. Use the same units in each entry so the record is easier to read later.

3

Save height and weight separately

Give each measurement its own labeled entry. This keeps the timeline clear and reduces the chance of mixing two numbers together.

4

Look at the trend, not one percentile

A single point does not tell the whole story. The American Academy of Pediatrics notes that a child's pattern over time matters more than one percentile number.

5

Write down questions for the next visit

Keep questions beside the record so they are easy to find. A pediatrician can interpret the measurements with the rest of the child's health information.

Before you enter a number

Keep the date and unit with it

A useful record keeps the source clear. Leave unclear details unresolved rather than guessing.

Copy-ready record template:
Measurement date: __________
Height or weight: __________
Value and unit exactly as recorded: __________
Source / context: __________

Example of context, not a child’s measurements: October 1, 2026 · height · cm · clinician’s record. Copy the actual value from your own source; no growth result or interpretation is implied.

Important context

A chart is not a diagnosis

The CDC says growth charts are one part of the overall health picture and should not be used by themselves to diagnose a condition. Ask a pediatrician about measurements or changes that concern you.
