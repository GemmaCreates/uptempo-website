Beginner guide

How to choose a beginner 5K training plan

A useful plan should tell you what to do today, leave room to walk and rest, and explain how to continue when life interrupts.

1

Start from your current ability

Look for a plan that asks what you can comfortably do now. Some beginners need more walking before adding short running intervals. The race date matters too, but it should not erase your starting point.

2

Treat the number of weeks as a guide

Beginner plans use different timelines. The NHS plan uses nine weeks, while Mayo Clinic publishes a seven-week schedule. Choose a plan you can adjust rather than assuming one calendar fits everyone.

3

Make sure walking is part of the plan

Run-walk intervals are a normal way to begin. They also give you a clear workout to follow without chasing a pace. A first 5K can include walking too.

4

Look for rest between run days

The NHS plan places a rest day between runs. Your plan should make recovery visible instead of presenting every open day as another workout.

5

Read the missed-run rule now

Find out whether you can move a workout, repeat a week, or continue from where you are. The answer should be clear before you miss a run, not hidden after the schedule breaks.

6

Check the price and tracking

Know what is free, what requires payment, and whether the charge repeats. Check whether the app needs an account, GPS, a watch, or a social profile if you want a simpler training tool.

Before you start

Listen to your body

This is general fitness information, not medical advice. The NHS advises speaking with a doctor if you are worried about your health before starting and stopping if something does not feel right. Seek professional help for symptoms that concern you.
