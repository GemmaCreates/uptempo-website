How to build a simple potty-time routine

Keep the cue, the bathroom steps, and the response familiar. The goal is a routine your family can repeat, not a perfect day.

1

Choose familiar moments

Start with a few natural transitions that already happen in your day, such as after waking, after a meal, or before leaving home. Pick moments that fit your child and family instead of filling the day with alerts.

2

Use the same short sequence

Use the same simple words and order each time: go to the bathroom, manage clothing, sit or try, wipe, flush, and wash hands. A familiar order makes the next step easier to understand.

3

Give each try a clear ending

If a timer helps, use it as a short and predictable boundary for the try. It should not force a child to stay when they are uncomfortable. Finish with the same cleanup steps whether or not anything happened.

4

Keep the response calm

Notice the effort to follow the routine. A simple “You tried” or a sticker can mark the moment without turning every result into a test. Handle misses and accidents without shame.

5

Share the routine with caregivers

Write down the cue words and basic steps for anyone helping your child. The exact timing can change, but the sequence and tone can stay familiar across home, daycare, and visits.

Keep in mind

This is a routine template

It is not medical advice or a readiness test. Ask your child’s pediatrician about pain, constipation, withholding, repeated distress, or questions about when to begin.

Routine references

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends keeping the bathroom place and sequence consistent. The CDC also advises families to choose routines that work for their own household.
